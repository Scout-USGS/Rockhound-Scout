Find rockhounding hotspots along Texas highways

Rockhound Scout checks published geologic map data along state and U.S. highways, finds the stretches where one rock formation meets another, prioritizes drainage crossings, and writes you a field guide entry (Hotspot Card) for each one: where to look, what to watch for from the truck, and what you might find.

The app itself: pick a region, tap Find My Hotspots, and get back a real hotspot card — this one for the Baby Head Ridge Llanite Dike Roadcut, one of the only sources of llanite on Earth.

How it works

  1. Pick a region or type your own Custom area. Choose a preset Texas region, or use Custom to type a town, area, or a plain sentence like “within 20 miles of my hotel at [address]” or “agatized petrified wood near Bryan, TX.”
  2. The app reads the maps. It finds nearby state and U.S. highways, samples the geologic map along each road, and prioritizes named drainages. Those contacts are where minerals, fossils, and petrified wood often concentrate.
  3. You get hotspot cards. Each card gives the formations on either side of the contact, named creeks nearby, driving directions from a nearby town, what to look for from the truck window, likely finds, and a Waze search term.

Straight talk about what this is

This is a research tool, not a guarantee. Geologic maps can’t show every small vein or outcrop, so a marked stretch is a starting point: check both sides of the road nearby. Most Texas land is privately owned, so always get the landowner’s permission before collecting, park off the highway shoulder, and follow all traffic and land-use laws. Questions

Is this a subscription?

No. It’s a one-time $25 purchase for 200 scans. Nothing renews and there’s nothing to cancel.

Do scans expire?

No. Use them whenever you like.

What counts as a scan?

One tap of Find My Hotspots, for a preset region or a Custom search. If the scan ends in an error or returns no hotspots, it isn’t deducted.

Can I get a refund?

We don’t refund unused scans or change-of-mind purchases. If we charge you by mistake, a scan is wrongly deducted, or the app is broken for an extended period, we’ll make it right. Details are in the Refund & Dispute Policy.

Does it cover places outside Texas?

Not yet. Rockhound Scout currently works only for Texas.

Where does the data come from?

Rock formation data comes from Macrostrat, a public database that compiles published geologic maps from sources including the U.S. Geological Survey and the Texas Bureau of Economic Geology. Roads and creek names come from OpenStreetMap. AI is used only to write the descriptions.
